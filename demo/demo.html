At your first visit, you can ask for extra time, ask the dentist to explain each step before it happens, and ask for a break whenever you need one. The goal of the first visit is simple: find out where your mouth stands, fix anything that hurts, and make a plan you can live with.

What actually happens at that first visit? You fill in a short history form, including medicines and allergies. You meet the team. The dentist examines your teeth and gums, takes X-rays if they are due, and talks through what they find. If a tooth needs work, they will explain the options, the cost, and how long it takes, and they will put it in writing. You are not asked to decide on the spot. Many returning patients are surprised by how little is wrong, and how much of the visit is just a cleaning and a conversation. The eight years of worry are usually worse than the appointment itself.

General Dentistry

General dentistry is where a returning patient starts again: an exam, a cleaning, and a look at the X-rays. The hygienist will take time with the cleaning, because tartar builds up over years, and they will be gentle about it. The dentist will check for cavities, gum disease, and worn teeth, and will show you the X-rays so you can see what they see. If you grind your teeth at night, they will look for the wear and talk about a night guard. That first cleaning can feel like a fresh start, and for many people it is the visit that makes the second one easy to book.

Emergency

Sometimes the return to the dentist is forced by pain, and that is fine too. A toothache, a broken tooth, or swelling that will not go away are all reasons to call now, not next month. The office keeps time for urgent cases, and they will tell you honestly how soon you need to be seen. If a tooth is loose, cracked, or bleeding, call (561) 260-5575 and describe what happened. The team will give you home steps to hold you until your visit, and they will fit you in as soon as they can. Pain is not a punishment for staying away. It is a signal, and the clinic treats it that way.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Once the pain is gone and the health side is in order, many returning patients ask about how their smile looks. Years of coffee, tea, or smoking can stain teeth, and a chip that was never fixed can still be there. Whitening, bonding, and veneers are all options, and the team will talk through them with real prices and real timeframes. There is no pressure to fix everything at once. Some people whiten first and see how they feel. Others fix one front tooth and leave the rest. The point is that the choice is yours, and it can wait until you are comfortable.

Dental Implants

Losing a tooth years ago and never replacing it is common, and it is never too late to talk about it. An implant replaces the root with a post, and a crown on top acts as the tooth. The process takes months, and the clinic will explain each stage, the cost, and what to expect. They will also talk about bridges and partial dentures, so you can compare. If you have lived with a gap for years, the biggest change is often how you eat, not just how you look. The team will check bone and gum health first, because that decides whether an implant is a good option for you.

Invisalign

Teeth shift over the years, especially after a tooth is lost, and many adults come back wanting to straighten them. Invisalign clear aligners move teeth a little at a time, and you take them out to eat and brush. The clinic will tell you how many trays your case is likely to need and what it will cost, before you commit. Aligners work best when worn most of the day, and they suit adults who want to fix crowding or a gap without brackets. If aligners cannot do the job, the team will say so, rather than start a treatment that cannot finish it.

Oral Surgery

Some returning patients need a tooth removed, often a wisdom tooth or a tooth that has cracked below the gum line. That work is done in the same office, by people who already know your history and how you handle numbing. They will explain the procedure, what to expect afterward, and how to care for the area, and they will check on you the next day. It is not a fun visit, but it is a planned one, and the team will go at your pace. If you are nervous about surgery, say so when you book, so they can allow extra time.

Frequently Asked Questions

I have not seen a dentist in years. Will I be judged?

No. The team sees returning patients all the time, and they will not lecture you. The first visit is about finding out where things stand and making a plan, not about the past.

Will my first visit hurt?

An exam and a cleaning should not hurt, though a deep cleaning can be sore for a day. Tell the team if you are nervous, and they will go slowly, explain each step, and stop whenever you need a break.

What if I need a lot of work?

The dentist will write a plan with the cost of each stage, and you can do the urgent work first. Financing is available through CareCredit and Cherry, and the team will help you spread the work out over time.

Can I bring someone with me?

Yes. Many nervous patients bring a family member or friend to the first visit. They can wait in the reception area or, if you like, come into the room for the exam.

Do you offer anything to help with anxiety?

Talk to the team when you book. They can allow extra time, explain everything in advance, and go at your pace. Let them know what worries you most, so they can plan around it.

How often will I need to come back?

Most adults do well with a checkup and cleaning every six months. If you have gum disease or a lot of work to do, the dentist may suggest more frequent visits at first, then back to six months.

What should I do about a tooth that hurts right now?

Call the office and describe the pain. They will advise on pain relief, tell you what to watch for, and fit you in as soon as they can, often the same day for severe pain or swelling.
